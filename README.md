# Thaeryzovhal

> A staircase of rain climbs toward the name hidden beneath a sleeping wing.

## The Umbrella Maker's Shop

The shop opens only while rain is falling somewhere beyond the town. Its owner knows when to turn the sign because a blue umbrella beside the door begins to drip. The pavement outside may remain dry for weeks.

Each umbrella is made from a different kind of silence. One opens with the silence of an empty church. Another contains the hush before a child's first word. The blue umbrella carries the silence left after a name has been called into a valley without receiving an echo.

The maker keeps it closed. Its handle is white horn, carved into the shape of a long sleeping animal. Every year another scale appears along the carving, although the handle remains the same size.

Customers sometimes ask its price. The maker tells them the weather has not yet decided.

## A Visitor From a Dry Season

A woman came to the shop with a damp letter. She had written it to a brother who lived beyond the mountains. It had returned without an address, without a stamp, and with rainwater sealed between its pages.

The umbrella maker held it beneath the blue umbrella. Drops rose from the letter and disappeared into the folded fabric. As they moved, the woman heard footsteps climbing wooden stairs.

She remembered those footsteps from the house where they had grown up. Her brother had always climbed slowly, counting each step in a language she had never learned. She had not thought of that language for many years.

The maker asked her to leave the letter overnight. She refused to part with it, so he placed a chair beside the workbench and brought a second cup of tea.

## The Rain That Chose a Direction

At midnight, the blue umbrella opened by itself. No rain fell beneath it. Instead, clear steps appeared one above another, rising through its fabric into a place beyond the shop's ceiling.

Each step held a tiny reflection. In the first, the woman saw her childhood kitchen. In the second, a mountain road. In the third, a room she did not recognize, with a coat hanging beside an open window.

The maker told her to climb only while holding the letter flat. If she folded it, the stairs would remember another direction. She asked whether he had climbed them himself. He showed her his hands, marked with narrow white lines like the creases of old paper.

She stepped onto the rain. It held her weight without breaking. Behind her, the shop grew quiet enough for her to hear the horn animal on the umbrella handle begin to breathe.

## The House Above the Weather

The staircase ended at a house built from pale stone. Rain rose around its walls and entered the clouds overhead. Through the open window, the woman saw the coat from the third step. Its pockets were full of small white feathers.

Her brother sat at a table, facing away. Before him lay a letter that looked exactly like hers. As she entered, he lifted one hand, but did not turn.

Beyond the far wall slept a creature with wings folded across the sky. Its scales resembled roof tiles. Its neck curved around the house, and its closed eye occupied the place where a second window should have been.

The woman understood the footsteps she had heard. Every step her brother had counted as a child had belonged to the rain staircase, though neither had known where it would eventually lead.

## The Letter's Other Side

She laid her letter beside his. Water moved between the two pages. Words appeared along their adjoining edges:

*Thae — ryzo — vhal — emreth.*

Her brother read them in the language of the staircase. The sleeping creature opened its eye. In it, the woman saw the umbrella maker standing beneath the blue fabric, holding both cups of tea as if afraid one might fall.

Her brother finally turned. His face was older than she remembered, but his sleeve still carried the little repair she had sewn before his last journey. He placed a white feather across the letters.

The feather absorbed the water. The pages became a single dry sheet. Outside, the rising rain paused, each drop holding a tiny house inside its clear surface.

## What Returned With the Umbrella

They walked to the staircase together. At its highest step, her brother stopped and gave her his coat. It was light despite the feathers in its pockets. He said nothing she could repeat afterward, though she remembered the warmth of his hand against her own.

The staircase descended through the umbrella. The maker was waiting with the tea, still warm. The woman stepped into the shop carrying a dry letter and a coat that smelled faintly of mountain air.

The blue umbrella closed. On its horn handle, the carved animal had folded one wing over its eye.

Since that night, the shop opens whenever the woman passes its door, regardless of the weather.

She has never bought an umbrella.

But when rain begins beyond the town, she sometimes hears a familiar voice counting the distance between one drop and the next.
